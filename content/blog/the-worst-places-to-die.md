We fact-checked 1,041,387 medical migrants from 39 countries in 2020/21. Twelve countries stood out: **the Philippines, Nigeria, Pakistan, India, Zimbabwe, Bangladesh, Nepal, Ghana, Kenya, Jamaica, Ethiopia, and South Africa.**

They are the world's biggest exporters of healthcare workers, in order of magnitude. And our families back home pay the highest cost for end-of-life care while getting the worst quality of death.

## The export/death paradox

The countries that train and send the carers the world depends on are the same countries where dying well is hardest. A nurse can deliver a good death every working day abroad — and still be unable to secure one for a parent at home.

Our free course [*The Worst Places to Die*](https://awholefamilymatter.com/spaces/23923002/content) asks **where** that suffering concentrates, and why in these twelve countries — and not other poor countries. It walks through **ten structural factors** and the **three feedback loops** that lock them together, including which factors are most acute in your own origin country.

## Death debt is structural

Approximately **85 million people** across just these twelve countries are pushed into poverty by individual healthcare spending. The course puts a number on the total cost of dying — and shows why death debt is structural, rather than a family's failure.

## The suffering is growing

The number of people dying with unaddressed severe pain is estimated to multiply two and a half times — to **20.1 million** a year by 2060.

## Who these countries share

All twelve share collectivist family values: an individual does not face mortality alone, end-of-life decisions are deeply relational, and generations are financially interdependent. That is exactly why care planning built for a single patient doesn't fit — and why we build for the whole family.
