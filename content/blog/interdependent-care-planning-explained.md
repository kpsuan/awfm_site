Advance care planning has traditionally been done on paper: one person fills in a form, signs it, and files it away. For collectivist families — where the family, not only the patient, decides — that model leaves almost everyone out.

**Interdependent Care Planning (ICP)** is our answer. It brings your immediate family, your family back home, and your local or chosen family into one place to plan, fund, and advocate for dignified end-of-life care — together.

## It starts with a question

ICP is a conversation, not a form, and it starts with a question. Based on the Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act (UHCDA) 2023 and collaboration with subject matter experts, we crafted questions that lead to love — so a whole family can deliver dignified end-of-life experiences together.

Every question moves through three checkpoints:

1. **Your position** — where you stand today. Asking a question such as *"How do you really feel about death and dying?"* begins here.
2. **Your challenges** — the same follow-up comes with every question: *"What concerns, issues, and challenges might you be facing?"*
3. **Your mind-changer** — *"What would make you change your mind?"* — the question that keeps the conversation honest.

At every checkpoint, family members choose from options supported by medical and legal, relational and social education on systemic end-of-life suffering — generated with AI and grounded in the lived experiences of medical migrants and their collectivist families. Then each person can explain their reasoning by **video, audio, or text**, for collective discussion and team affirmation.

## Funding care together

Pooling money is something our cultures already know — *paluwagan* in the Philippines, *esusu* in Nigeria. Instead of reactionary cash remittances during a family emergency, care teams proactively pool for each other's end-of-life care through five purpose-based pools: urgent, quality of life, legacy and rites, repatriation, and mutual aid.

## Learning to speak for each other

Patient advocacy learning formalises palliative care workbooks into seven stages of a care team's journey, based on the 7 keys framework we learnt directly from the authors of *Hope for the Best, Plan for the Rest* — Dr. Sammy Winemaker and Dr. Hsien Seow.
