Because the family — not the patient — makes decisions in our collectivist cultures, **77 to 98 percent of our terminal patients don't know they're dying.** They are kept safe from the truth by families who love them and care for them.

That protection is an act of love. It is also how a terminal diagnosis vanishes somewhere between the family, the physician, and a system with no room for the conversation. Advance care planning is always too early — until it's too late.

## You live on both sides of it

If you're a medical migrant, you know this from both sides. You work a shift in London or Paris, Rotterdam or Lisbon. You deliver a good death every working day. Then you call home — and your family is dying inside a system that does not have what you have.

## Why the silence persists

The silence is a documented phenomenon, not a stereotype. And migration does not dissolve it. Family harmony is protected at all costs, often resulting in an intense, protective silence around terminal diagnoses and financial strain — to spare loved ones from distress.

## Breaking it together

Breaking the silence doesn't mean overriding the family. It means giving the whole family a way to have the conversation together — especially when they disagree. That is what every question in A Whole Family Matter is designed to do: each family member states where they stand, names what's hard, and says what would change their mind.

Our free community course, [*The Conspiracy of Silence*](https://awholefamilymatter.com/spaces/23296608/content), goes deeper: why diaspora families suffer in silence at end of life, and what it would take to make the words *"my family needs to plan"* sayable.
